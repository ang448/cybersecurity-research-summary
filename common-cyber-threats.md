### Common Cyber Security Threats
Cyber Threats are activities or patterns which could cause damage to data & systems (Confidentiality/Integrity/Availability).
#### 1. Phishing

Phishing is a form of social engineering used to trick users into giving away information or putting them at risk. This can be done via emails, messages, calls and websites which are misleading and can have warning signs such as unexpected requests, pressure to act fast, unknown senders and dubiouls links.
#### 2. Malware

This is any type of software that can disrupt your device, gather information or provide unauthorised access. This includes viruses, worms, trojans and spyware. Risk can be reduced by obtaining software from reliable sources along with updating any security you have.
#### 3. Ransomware

This category come under Malware. It can restrict your files/systems, often by encrypting data, and ask you to pay money. Having a reliable back up, keeping something updated, having access and having an incident plan can help users prepare for this threat.
#### 4. Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

This attack tries to overwhelm an online service. This can cause your website/service to run slowly or be unusable. However organisations can monitor any traffic or use a specialist security company for DDoS.
#### 5. Social Engineering

This is when users are manipulated into giving away information or doing something to put themselves at risk. This could be done via emails, messages, calls or face to face. Taking verification steps and having training can help reduce risk.
#### 6. Password Attacks

Sometimes attackers will use duplicated, weak or leaked passwords to gain access to your accounts. This can be restricted by using unique passwords, using a password manager and turning on multi-factor auth.
### Summary

Cyber threats can affect technological devices as well as human’s. However by knowing warning signs, keeping systems up to date and reporting something suspicious can help protect against these threats.
